## Narration

Silicon is the second most common element in Earth's crust, found in sand and most rocks. For electronics, it is purified until almost every atom is silicon, then sliced into thin wafers. Silicon is a semiconductor, so it can switch electricity on and off, the job of the billions of tiny switches inside a computer chip. Most solar panels also use silicon to turn sunlight into electricity. And sand, made largely of silicon and oxygen, is melted to make glass. From the beach to your pocket.

## Accuracy notes

Check the generated footage against these before publishing:

- Do not confuse silicon (the element) with silicone (rubbery polymers made from it).
- Electronic-grade silicon is typically better than 99.9999999% pure.
- Pure silicon is a shiny grey solid, not a sand-colored one.
