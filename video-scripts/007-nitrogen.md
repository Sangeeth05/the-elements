- **Length:** 40 seconds, 80 words of narration
- **Scenes:** 6
- **Style prompt (add to every scene):** Photorealistic and cinematic, soft studio lighting, deep navy-to-black backgrounds, shallow depth of field, slow and smooth camera moves, 16:9. No text, captions, logos or watermarks in the frame (titles are added in editing).

## Narration

Take a breath: about seventy-eight percent of the air you breathe is nitrogen. Its two atoms are held together by a very strong triple bond, so it rarely reacts. Factories break that bond to make ammonia, the basis of fertilizers that help feed billions of people. Cooled to minus one hundred ninety-six degrees Celsius, it becomes a liquid used to freeze and store biological samples. It also fills snack bags, keeping out oxygen so food stays fresh. Invisible, but everywhere.

## Accuracy notes

Check the generated footage against these before publishing:

- Nitrogen gas and liquid nitrogen are both colorless; the white "smoke" is condensed water vapor from the air.
- Liquid nitrogen boils at about −196 °C (77 K).
- The "feeds billions" phrasing is deliberately general; avoid quoting an exact share of the world population.
