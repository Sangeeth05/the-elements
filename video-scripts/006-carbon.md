- **Length:** 36 seconds, 76 words of narration

## Narration

Graphite and diamond look nothing alike, yet both are made of pure carbon. The difference is how the atoms are arranged: soft, slippery layers in graphite, and a rigid, tightly bonded network in diamond. That is why graphite works in pencils, while industrial diamonds cut through stone. Carbon also turns iron into steel, the backbone of our buildings and bridges. And activated carbon, full of tiny pores, filters our water and air. One element, many forms.

## Accuracy notes

Check the generated footage against these before publishing:

- Pencil "lead" contains no lead: it is graphite mixed with clay.
- Industrial diamonds are usually small and often synthetic; avoid showing large gem-quality stones on tools.
- Steel typically contains well under 2% carbon; do not imply it is mostly carbon.
