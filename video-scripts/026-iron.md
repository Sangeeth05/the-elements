- **Length:** 40 seconds, 82 words of narration
- **Scenes:** 6
- **Style prompt (add to every scene):** Photorealistic and cinematic, soft studio lighting, deep navy-to-black backgrounds, shallow depth of field, slow and smooth camera moves, 16:9. No text, captions, logos or watermarks in the frame (titles are added in editing).

## Narration

Iron is the metal we use more than any other, and most of Earth's core is made of it. Iron was forged inside massive stars and scattered across space when they exploded. Add a little carbon and iron becomes steel, which builds skyscrapers, bridges, cars and ships. Left in damp air, though, iron slowly reacts with oxygen and turns to rust. Inside your body, iron sits at the heart of hemoglobin, carrying oxygen in your blood. From star cores to your bloodstream.

## Accuracy notes

Check the generated footage against these before publishing:

- Earth's core is mostly iron with some nickel.
- Steel usually contains well under 2% carbon.
- Rust needs both oxygen and water; dry air alone rusts iron very slowly.
