## Narration

Aluminium is the most common metal in Earth's crust, but it is never found pure in nature. It is extracted from an ore called bauxite, using huge amounts of electricity. Light and strong, aluminium alloys help build planes, trains and cars. A thin natural oxide layer protects it from rusting, which makes it perfect for cans and foil. Recycling aluminium uses only a small fraction of the energy needed to make it new. Light metal, heavy lifting.

## Accuracy notes

Check the generated footage against these before publishing:

- Aluminium does not rust like iron; it forms a thin, protective oxide layer.
- Commonly cited figure: recycling saves up to about 95% of the energy of primary production. The narration keeps it general.
- The narration uses the "aluminium" spelling; use "aluminum" consistently instead if recording for a US audience.
