## Narration

This orange-red glow is neon, a noble gas that lights up when electricity passes through it. Neon is rare in air. It is collected by cooling air until it turns liquid, then separating the gases. Its glow gave neon signs their name, although other colors usually come from different gases or coated tubes. Mixed with helium, neon makes the red beam of helium-neon lasers. It is also a key gas in the ultraviolet lasers used to print patterns on computer chips. A rare gas with a bright future.

## Accuracy notes

Check the generated footage against these before publishing:

- Only red-orange signs typically contain pure neon; do not label blue or green signs as neon.
- Neon makes up roughly 0.0018% of air.
- In chipmaking lasers, neon is the buffer gas; the ultraviolet light comes from argon fluoride or krypton fluoride.
