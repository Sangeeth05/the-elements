## Narration

Helium is a light, colorless noble gas that almost never reacts with anything. It was first discovered in sunlight, before anyone found it on Earth. Liquid helium, just four degrees above absolute zero, keeps the powerful magnets in MRI scanners cold. Because it is lighter than air and does not burn, helium lifts balloons and airships safely. Deep-sea divers breathe helium mixtures to avoid the dizzy effects of nitrogen at great depths. Light, calm, and surprisingly hard to replace.

## Accuracy notes

Check the generated footage against these before publishing:

- Helium gas is invisible; it only glows inside a discharge tube with electricity applied.
- Liquid helium boils at about 4 K (−269 °C). It is a clear liquid, not a glowing one.
- Helium on Earth comes mainly from natural gas wells, where it builds up from radioactive decay underground.
