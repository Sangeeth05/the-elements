## Narration

Sodium is a soft, silvery metal, so soft you can cut it with a knife. It reacts strongly with water and air, so pure sodium is stored under oil. Combined with chlorine it becomes something far friendlier: table salt, used to season and preserve food. Sodium vapor lamps gave many streets their warm yellow-orange glow. Baking soda helps cakes rise, and sodium ions help carry signals along our nerves. Wild alone, essential in compounds.

## Accuracy notes

Check the generated footage against these before publishing:

- Do not show sodium being dropped into water at close range; if shown at all, it must be a tiny piece behind a safety shield.
- Table salt is sodium chloride, not sodium metal.
- Many cities have replaced sodium lamps with LEDs, so use the past or "many streets" phrasing.
