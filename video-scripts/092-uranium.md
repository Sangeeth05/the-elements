# Uranium (U, 92): video script

- **Length:** 41 seconds, 85 words of narration
- **Scenes:** 6
- **Style prompt (add to every scene):** Photorealistic and cinematic, soft studio lighting, deep navy-to-black backgrounds, shallow depth of field, slow and smooth camera moves, 16:9. No text, captions, logos or watermarks in the frame (titles are added in editing).

## Narration

Uranium is the heaviest element found naturally in large amounts, and it is slightly radioactive. When certain uranium atoms split, they release a huge amount of energy. In nuclear power plants, a single fuel pellet the size of a fingertip holds roughly as much energy as a tonne of coal. That energy powers cities, and some submarines and aircraft carriers. Because uranium decays into lead at a steady rate, it also helps scientists measure the age of rocks. A heavy element with a long history.

## Accuracy notes

Check the generated footage against these before publishing:

- Do not show explosions, mushroom clouds or weapons.
- Cooling towers release water vapor, not smoke or radiation.
- The "one pellet ≈ one tonne of coal" comparison is a commonly cited approximation; keep the word "roughly".
- Yellowcake is a uranium oxide concentrate, not pure uranium metal.
